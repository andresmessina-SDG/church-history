Era X · AD 1900–today

The Global Church

“A great multitude that no one could number, from every nation, from all tribes and peoples and languages.”

Revelation 7:9

Christianity’s centre of gravity moves south and east. Pentecostal and evangelical movements spread rapidly, the church in China grows under persecution, and most Christians now live in Africa, Asia and Latin America while the West grows secular.

Totalitarian states persecute the church and secular cultures sideline it. The church grows where it is weakest in worldly terms.

Opening

The long story of the Christian Church is a panorama of lights and shadows, of achievement and failure, of conquests and divisions. It has exhibited the divine life marvellously transforming the lives of men. It has also exhibited those passions and weaknesses of which human nature is capable.

Williston Walker, A History of the Christian Church §XVI, conclusion. 1918. Read the whole

Its tasks have seemed, in every age, almost insuperable. They were never greater than at present when confronted by a materialistic interpretation of life, and when the furnace of almost universal war bids fair to transform the whole fabric of European and American civilization. Yet no Christian can survey what the church has done without confidence in its future.

Williston Walker, A History of the Christian Church §XVI, conclusion. 1918. Read the whole

Its changes may be many, its struggles great. But the good hand of God which has led it hitherto will guide it to larger usefulness in the advancement of the kingdom of its Lord, and toward the fulfilment of His prediction that if He be lifted up He would draw all men unto Him.

People

  • Herman Bavinck 1854–1921 — Dutch Reformed theologian
  • J. Gresham Machen 1881–1937 — Princeton scholar, founder of Westminster Seminary
